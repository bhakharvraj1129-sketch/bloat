 2. The concerns behind the changes
 Without a clinician, Bloat cannot make any medical judgment. Three things go wrong if it does:

  • Safety. If the app judges wrongly, a real emergency could be missed or healthy people
    frightened. With no qualified person checking the logic, the error rests on you.
  • Medical-device law. In January 2026 the US FDA updated its general wellness guidance. A
    product stops being wellness if it is meant to diagnose, treat or prevent a condition, prompts or
    guides specific medical action, names a specific condition when suggesting a doctor, calls
    outputs abnormal, uses clinical thresholds, or gives ongoing alerts to manage a disease. Showing
    values, trends and summaries is allowed. Health Canada and India's CDSCO also judge software
    by its intended use and claims.
  • Advertising law. The US FTC requires competent and reliable scientific evidence for
    health-benefit claims. India's Consumer Protection Act covers misleading advertisements.
 Separately, health data is regulated whether or not a clinician is involved: the FTC Health Breach
 Notification Rule and state laws such as Washington's My Health My Data Act (first lawsuit filed in
 February 2025) in the USA, PIPEDA and Quebec Law 25 in Canada, and the DPDP Act in India.

 Every change in this record does one of four things: removes a medical judgment; replaces an
 expert check with a published source or a test you can run; protects health data; or records a
 founder decision.

 4. Safety: from questions to an information page
 This is the largest group of changes. All of them follow from one rule: the app may give information
 about when to get help, but it may not decide who needs help.

 C7. 19 safety questions removed · Concern: no clinician review / Concern:
 medical-device law

 v4.1 (before)     Before sign-up and in onboarding the app asked 19 Yes / No / Not sure questions (SAF-01 to
                   SAF-19), for example severe sudden pain, vomiting blood, fever, black stool, lump in the
                   tummy, unable to pee. Each answer set a route: EMERGENCY, URGENT, URGENT_INFO,
                   ROUTINE_ASSESSMENT or UNRESOLVED.

 v4.2 (after)      No safety questions. Replaced by one fixed page, “When to get medical help” (section 5),
                   listing the same warning signs as information.

 Why               Asking about symptoms and sorting people by urgency is triage, a clinical function. Deciding
                   which symptom is “emergency” versus “urgent” is a doctor's job; a wrong route either misses
                   a real emergency or alarms healthy users. A fixed page gives the same information but the
                   user reads it and decides. The FDA has long treated information on when to consult a doctor as
                   low risk. The wording is based on the NHS bloating page, so a health authority wrote it, not
                   you.

 C8. Safety help pop-up removed · Concern: no clinician review
 v4.1 (before)     Three pop-up cards: Emergency (dark red, call button, opens instantly on tap), Urgent (“see a
                   doctor today or soon”), Get advice soon.

 v4.2 (after)      No pop-ups. The help page is always one tap away.

 Why               The pop-ups only existed to show the result of the triage. With no triage there is nothing to
                   show.

 C9. Routine health check screen removed · Concern: no clinician review
 v4.1 (before)     Onboarding screen with SAF-11 to SAF-16; a Yes showed “Worth booking a check-up”.

 v4.2 (after)      Removed. A permanent line on the help page and in Me: “If bloating is severe, persistent or
                   worrying, talk to a doctor.”

 Why               Judging who should book a check-up is the same clinical decision in a milder form.

 C10. No “all clear” anywhere · Concern: no clinician review / Concern: medical-device law
 v4.1 (before)     Answering No to all questions let the user continue as if cleared; a Not sure answer blocked
                   any all-clear.

 v4.2 (after)      The only button is “I've read this”. No screen says or implies “all clear” or “no warning signs”.
                   New test Q93 checks this.

 Why               An all-clear tells the user the app checked them and found them safe. If that user were
                   seriously ill, the app made a false medical statement.

 C11. Insights never pause · Concern: no clinician review
 v4.1 (before)     A Yes or Not sure on an urgent sign paused comparisons, summaries and tips (“interpretation
                   holds”) until a versioned policy released them. SAF-18 had an interim rule that did not pause.

 v4.2 (after)      Nothing pauses. The holds table and the SAF-18 interim rule are removed.

 Why               The pause was triggered by triage. Without triage there is nothing to trigger it, and deciding
                   when it is safe to resume was itself a clinical rule.

 C12. No symptom check when logging an episode · Concern: no clinician review
 v4.1 (before)     Picking tummy pain or nausea on “Anything else with it?” (EP-08) offered a reviewed symptom
                   check. The same applied on the onboarding symptoms screen.

 v4.2 (after)      No check. A small “When to get medical help” link sits under the symptom chips.

 Why               That check was triage at a different moment.

 C13. No thresholds or alerts · Concern: medical-device law / Concern: no clinician review
 v4.1 (before)     Planned: escalation such as “scores of 8 or more on many days → see a doctor”, still to be set
                   by a clinician.

 v4.2 (after)      No alerts based on score size or frequency. No “high”, “abnormal” or “concerning” labels.
                   Scores are shown as numbers and charts only. New test Q95.

 Why               The FDA's 2026 guidance names clinical thresholds and ongoing alerts as features that take a
                   product out of wellness. Choosing the number and the number of days is a medical decision.

 C14. Bloat never names a condition · Concern: medical-device law / Concern:
 advertising law

 v4.1 (before)     No explicit rule.

 v4.2 (after)      No screen, card, summary, export or PDF generated by Bloat may suggest a condition (e.g.
                   “this may be IBS or lactose intolerance”). Condition names appear only where the user entered
                   them. New test Q96.

 Why               Suggesting a condition is diagnosis. The FDA lists naming a specific condition as a disqualifier
                   for wellness, and it is the claim most likely to mislead a user.

 C15. Personal Focus P2, P3 and P4 removed · Concern: no clinician review
 v4.1 (before)     P2: constipation, diarrhoea or no bowel movement on 3+ of 14 days → show the bowel card
                   first. P3: gas or burping on 3+ days → gum, straw and fizzy-drink tracking. P4: heartburn, or
                   most episodes after 6 pm → meal-timing tracking. All marked as needing clinical review.

 v4.2 (after)      Removed. P1 (order by cuisine), P5 (user's own suspicion), P6 (time-of-day pattern) and P7
                   (cycle shading) stay. The useful parts survive: the bowel log is available to all, and guidance
                   cards trigger from habits the user logged, not from symptoms.

 Why               Each rule turned a symptom pattern into an action, which is interpreting symptoms. v4.1 itself
                   said they needed a clinician.

 5. New page: “When to get medical help”
 This page replaces every safety question. It is identical for all users, asks nothing and stores only
 that it was viewed. Wording follows the NHS bloating page, adapted for the USA, Canada and India.

 C16. Help page shown before sign-up and always reachable · Concern: no
 clinician review

 v4.1 (before)      Safety check 1 (4 emergency questions) shown before sign-up.

 v4.2 (after)       The help page is shown before sign-up and requires a tap on “I've read this”. It is linked from
                    Log bloating, the onboarding symptoms screen, Me, experiment screens and the website
                    footer. Emergency number follows country: 911 for USA and Canada, 112 for India; “your local
                    emergency number” anywhere else.

 Why                It replaces the duty of care the safety questions provided: every user is told where to get help
                    before they start, and can find it from any symptom screen.

 Full page text (draft)
    When to get medical help

    Bloat is a tracker. It can't tell you what is causing your symptoms or whether they are serious.

    Call 911 (USA and Canada) or 112 (India) now if you have bloating with a swollen stomach and:
    stomach pain that is severe and came on very suddenly; you are vomiting blood, or your vomit looks
    like coffee grounds; severe difficulty breathing (gasping, choking or unable to get words out).

    Contact a doctor today or go to urgent care if you have bloating and: vomiting, diarrhoea or
    constipation; stomach pain; a high temperature, or you feel hot, cold or shivery; a swelling or lump
    in your tummy; you can't pee, poo or pass gas; a burning feeling in your chest from stomach acid.

    Book a regular appointment with a doctor if: you feel bloated regularly; you've tried changing
    what you eat and still keep feeling bloated; you have bloating and are losing weight without trying;
    you have bloating and blood in your poo.

    Source: NHS, “Bloating”. This page is general information, not medical advice. [ I've read this ]

 Stored: date and time viewed, page version, acknowledged yes or no. Sign-up continues normally after the tap;
 nothing is blocked or paused.

 6. Personal experiments
 C17. Experiments become habit-only and move to the first release ·
 Concern: no clinician review / Founder decision

 v4.1 (before)     Stage S3 “food observation programme”: clinician-supervised food tests with 7 eligibility
                   questions (FT-01 to FT-07: age, pregnancy, eating disorder, allergy, IBD or coeliac, weight loss,
                   clinician approval). Off by default.

 v4.2 (after)      Pro, first release. Five habit experiments only: eat more slowly; no chewing gum or hard
                   candy; no straws; fewer fizzy drinks; a gentle 10-minute walk after lunch and dinner,
                   only if walking is comfortable. None removes a food, changes how much is eaten, or
                   involves supplements or fasting. FT-01 to FT-07 removed.

 Why               NIDDK, the AGA and Monash all say cutting foods (dairy, wheat, low-FODMAP) should be done
                   with a doctor or dietitian; done alone it can cause nutritional gaps and trigger eating disorders.
                   Even “eat smaller meals” carries that risk, and deciding who to exclude is a clinical call. The
                   five habits are listed by NIDDK and NHS as general advice for everyone, so nobody needs
                   screening.

 C18. Experiment rules · Concern: advertising law
 v4.1 (before)     None (feature was off).

 v4.2 (after)      7 days, compared with the 7 days before. One experiment at a time. Can't start unless 4+ of
                   the previous 7 days have a confirmed Bloat Score. Daily question “Did you do it today?” Yes /
                   Partly / No, on Today and in the morning review. Shown before starting: “This is a personal
                   observation, not a treatment. Stop any time if you feel worse.” and “If you have a diagnosed
                   condition, follow your doctor's advice first.”

 Why               Comparing against the user's own previous week is the simplest honest comparison. The 4-day
                   minimum stops results built on too little data.

 C19. Experiment results use fixed wording · Concern: advertising law
 v4.1 (before)     None.

 v4.2 (after)      With 4+ confirmed days in each period and 4+ “Yes” days: “During the experiment your
                   average Bloat Score was 3.9 (6 days). In the 7 days before it was 5.2 (7 days). This is a pattern
                   in your own records, not proof that the change caused it.” Otherwise: “There aren't enough
                   days to compare yet.” AI never writes or rewrites results.

 Why               With 2–3 days any difference is mostly chance. The fixed sentence stops the app making a
                   cause-and-effect claim, which the FTC would expect scientific proof for.

 C20. Ending early because “I felt worse” · Concern: no clinician review
 v4.1 (before)     None.

 v4.2 (after)      “End experiment early” with reasons: I felt worse / Too hard to keep up / Other. “I felt worse”
                   shows no numbers, shows the help link, and is logged for you.

 Why               A chart could nudge someone who feels worse to keep going. The log is your early-warning
                   system in place of a clinician.

 C49. Written intended-use statement · Concern: medical-device law
 v4.1 (before)     Product described as not diagnostic, spread across the master document.

 v4.1 (before)     Not drafted.

 C53. Marketing wording rules · Concern: advertising law
 v4.1 (before)     Positioning ideas only; earlier ideas included “bloat preventer”.

 v4.2 (after)      Allowed: “track your bloating in seconds”, “see patterns in your own records”, “notice what
                   tends to happen before you bloat”, “try simple habit experiments and see your results”, “bring

 15. What did not change

  • Bloat Score = confirmed worst bloating for a completed day, 0–10, only when the review says it
    covers the whole day; today shows “Highest logged so far”.

    never a guessed end time.
  • Day statuses: Confirmed, Provisional, Conflict, Late, Unknown. Review allowed until the end of
    the next day.
  • 7-day average needs at least 4 confirmed days.
  • Comparisons need at least 7 days with and 7 days without, with full meal records.
  • Unknown stays unknown; a missing tap is never “No”.

    standard error format.
  • Edit and delete contracts for episodes and meals (Addendum A6).
  • The 24 activities and their groups.
  • Download my data and delete account for everyone.
  • Slice A order: onboarding → episode → meal → yesterday review → history → correction → export,
    on synthetic data first.
